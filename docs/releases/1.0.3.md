# Wave Race 64: Recompiled 1.0.3

> **Withdrawn.** Southern Island became unplayable in this build. The release
> was removed and the water code reverted to 1.0.2's; see `CHANGELOG.md` and
> `docs/PORTING.md`, *1.0.3 withdrawn*.

* The distant sea has waves all the way to the horizon, instead of going flat
  partway out
* Fixed the flat edge of water that travelled with the camera on rough water
* Fixed the distant water shimmering and jittering
* Draw Distance now also widens the water the game actually simulates: at
  Extended the waves and their physics cover 78% more area, so waves travel and
  buoys bob further out. Original is unchanged from the cartridge
* Two players: the second screen now has sea past the edge of the game's own
  water, which it never had
* Water heights are slightly more accurate on steep waves

Known issues:
* On Linux, Water Quality above Original can crash with a heap corruption error
  after a minute or two. Not yet explained; Original is unaffected
* The wider simulated water at Extended is one player only

Not tested:
* macOS
* Linux on real hardware — the Linux build is only exercised on a software
  renderer here

You need your own dump of **Wave Race 64 (USA) (Rev A)**. Windows: unzip and run
`WaveRace64Recomp.exe`. Linux: extract and run `./WaveRace64Recomp.sh`.

## What was actually found

The release started as two issue reports about the sea stopping short (#12) and
a flat edge travelling with the camera (#19). It ended somewhere else, because
the premise both the port and its documentation had been working from was wrong.

**The game simulates water in a hexagon around the camera, and nowhere else.**
`func_80050F8C` re-centres a fixed cell window on the camera every frame and
`func_8004EAA4` integrates only that window. Its size sits in `D_800D4684` as a
hexagon cell count, `3N(N+1)+1`:

| scene | N | cells | live radius |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| opening / rider select | 16 | 817 | 1,024 |
| **one-player race** | **12** | **469** | **768** |
| two players | 10 | 331 | 640 |

For N = 12 that window is 24 x 24 cells = **1,536 x 1,330 world units**, which is
this project's own measured patch extent to the unit. The drawn patch *is* the
simulated hexagon — and the patch's outline being 100% pinned to y = 0, an
oddity recorded and unexplained since 1.0.1, is simply that window's boundary
ring.

Outside it nothing is integrated. Cells keep whatever a wave generator or a
passing craft last stamped. On a captured field, **84% of entries have a height
but only 27.7% have ever been stepped**. So the sea extension ring, which was
built on the premise that "the swell outside agrees with the swell inside", was
sampling a frozen snapshot — true of the array, false of its contents. What
looked like distant waves moving was the ring's own sampling pattern crawling
over dead data as the camera slid past at 48–57 units a frame.

## The distant shimmer had four causes, not one

Each was found by measurement or elimination, and each was hidden by the one
before it:

1. **Sampling water that was not alive.** Fixed by carrying field-derived waves
   only as far as the game simulates them, read from `D_80192494` each frame.
   This also stopped the ring reaching the wave field's **14,190-unit alias**,
   where three world cells share one array entry and the outer ring was reading
   the water under the boat.
2. **Point-sampling below the sample spacing.** The ring's neighbours are 112
   units apart at its inner edge against a 64-unit lattice. Samples are now
   filtered over their footprint, at no extra cost — the taps were already there.
3. **Heights quantised to whole world units.** A `Vtx` holds s16 coordinates, so
   a sliding vertex walked a 1-unit staircase at 20 fps — a quarter of the far
   swell's whole amplitude. Heights are carried in sixteenths now, with the
   ring's own matrix scaling Y back down.
4. **The swell's phase losing precision.** Written as `2*pi*(along - frame/period)`,
   with `along` about one and `frame/period` climbing without bound, a float's
   spacing at a few thousand is 5e-4 cycles — so the height advanced in about
   eight steps a frame, worsening the longer the game ran. Showing only on water
   holding a steady height, this one survived all three fixes above.

## Left out deliberately

**Simulated water beyond 16 cells.** 16 is where the cartridge's data ends: the
mesh's triangles are static display lists in ROM, one per window size, and the
port hands over the game's own N=16 list rather than generating geometry. Going
further needs a generated display list and three re-pointed buffers, for 1,280
units at N=20 against 1,024, with an `O(N^2)` integrator behind it. The recipe
is in `docs/PORTING.md` if that judgement is ever revisited.

**An attribution for issue #19.** The edge is confirmed gone, but no single
change was tested against it alone — and `fit_ring_to_patch`, the mechanism
written specifically to fix it, is **off** in the build where it disappeared. A
fix without an attribution is worth less than it looks; if the edge returns,
that is where to start.

**Issue #27.** The Linux heap aborts are unexplained. A bounds check landed on an
unchecked walk in the water renderer, but it is explicitly not the cause: the
mechanism that would have made it one required an unclamped GPU buffer write,
and `robustBufferAccess` is enabled wherever the driver supports it.
